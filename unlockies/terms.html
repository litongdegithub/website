1. What Unlockies does

Unlockies helps families manage screen time. A parent locks chosen apps on a child’s iPhone or iPad with Apple’s Screen Time and sets the rules. The child earns minutes by doing activities with five cartoon buddies, and spends them to unlock the locked apps for a while. Unlockies is set up and controlled by a parent, who agrees to these terms for the family. It doesn’t replace a parent’s supervision.

2. Screen Time and its limits

  • Unlockies works through Apple’s Screen Time and Family Sharing. Apple decides what can be locked, and some apps and system features can’t be. The child’s Apple Account must be in the parent’s Family Sharing.
  • We can’t guarantee that Unlockies blocks everything. A child who knows the device passcode or your Apple Account password, or a change to Screen Time or Family Sharing made outside the app, can get around the locks. iOS updates can also change how Screen Time works.
  • Keep the parent PIN, your Apple Account password and the device passcode to yourself.

3. Activity checks are estimates

Counting reps with the camera, telling when an instrument is played, recognising words read aloud, counting steps and checking that the device lies face down are all done automatically on the device. They are estimates: they can miss a rep, count one that wasn’t finished, or be fooled. Unlockies doesn’t measure fitness, musical skill or reading level, and makes no health or educational claims.

4. Exercise safety

Bo the Bear’s push-ups, squats and jumping jacks, and Pip the Corgi’s walks, are physical activities. Parents are responsible for deciding whether they suit their child and for supervising them.

  • Warm up first, and clear enough space around your child. Keep away from furniture, stairs and anything that could fall.
  • Place the device where it can’t fall, and don’t hold it while exercising.
  • Stop if anything hurts, or if your child feels dizzy or short of breath. Check with a doctor if your child has a health condition or injury.
  • On walks, follow the usual rules for your child’s age: an adult nearby where needed, and eyes up, not on the screen, near roads.
